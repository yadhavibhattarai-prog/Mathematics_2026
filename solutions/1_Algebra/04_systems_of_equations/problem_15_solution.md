# Exercise 15. Classifying Three Systems

For each of the following systems, perform only as much elimination as necessary to recognize its type:

**System 1**

$$
\begin{cases}
x+y=2, \\
x-y=0,
\end{cases}
$$

**System 2**

$$
\begin{cases}
x+y=2, \\
2x+2y=4,
\end{cases}
$$

**System 3**

$$
\begin{cases}
x+y=2, \\
2x+2y=5.
\end{cases}
$$

For each, justify the classification: one solution, infinitely many solutions, or no solution.

## Solution

- **System 1:** Adding the equations gives $2x=2$, so $x=1$ and then $y=1$. There is **one solution**, $(1,1)$.
- **System 2:** The second equation is twice the first, so it imposes no new condition. There are **infinitely many solutions**, $(x,y)=(t,2-t)$ for $t\in\mathbb{R}$.
- **System 3:** Doubling the first equation gives $2x+2y=4$, which contradicts the second equation $2x+2y=5$. There is **no solution**.
