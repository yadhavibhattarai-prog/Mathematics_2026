# Exercise 4. Parabola

For

$$
y^2=12x,
$$

identify the vertex, axis of symmetry, and opening direction. Find points on the parabola for $x=3$ and use them to make a sketch.

> **Why this exercise:** teaches how to move from the equation of a parabola to its basic geometry.

## Solution

### 1. Vertex, Axis of Symmetry, and Opening Direction

Comparing $y^2 = 12x$ with the standard canonical equation of a horizontal parabola $y^2 = 2px$:

- Parameter: $2p = 12 \implies p = 6$.
- **Vertex:** The origin $V = (0, 0)$.
- **Axis of Symmetry:** The line $y = 0$ ($x$-axis), because substituting $-y$ for $y$ leaves the equation invariant: $(-y)^2 = y^2 = 12x$.
- **Opening Direction:** Since $y^2 \ge 0$ for all real $y$, we must have $12x \ge 0 \implies x \ge 0$. Therefore, the parabola opens to the right (along the positive $x$-axis).
- **Focus and Directrix:** The focus is located at $(p/2, 0) = (3, 0)$, and the directrix is the vertical line $x = -p/2 = -3$.

### 2. Points on the Parabola for $x = 3$

Substitute $x = 3$ into the equation:

$$
y^2 = 12\cdot 3 = 36
$$

Taking the square root:

$$
y = \pm 6
$$

This yields two symmetric points on the curve:

$$
P_1 = (3, 6), \qquad P_2 = (3, -6)
$$

These two points lie on the focal chord perpendicular to the axis of symmetry (the *latus rectum*), confirming that the distance between them is $2p = 12$.
