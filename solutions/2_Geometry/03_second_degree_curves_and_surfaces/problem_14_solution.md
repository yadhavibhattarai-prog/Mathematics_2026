# Exercise 14. Cone and Cylinder

Compare

$$
x^2+y^2=z^2
$$

and

$$
x^2+y^2=4.
$$

For each surface, describe the cross-sections $z=c$, $x=0$, and $y=0$, make a sketch, and explain why the radius of the cross-section depends on $z$ in the first case but is constant in the second.

> **Why this exercise:** teaches how to distinguish a cone from a cylinder by the behavior of their cross-sections.

## Solution

### 1. Analysis of $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$ (Circular Cone)

#### Cross-Sections $z = c$
Substitute $z = c$ into the equation:

$$
x^2 + y^2 = c^2
$$

- For $c \neq 0$: This is a circle in the plane $z = c$, centered at $(0, 0, c)$, with radius

$$
R(c) = \sqrt{c^2} = |c|
$$

- For $c = 0$: The equation becomes $x^2 + y^2 = 0$, which gives the single point $(0, 0, 0)$ (the vertex or apex of the cone).
- The radius grows linearly with the vertical distance from the origin: moving away from the vertex expands the circular cross-section proportionally to $|c|$.

#### Cross-Section $x = 0$ ($yz$-plane)
Substitute $x = 0$:

$$
y^2 = z^2 \implies y^2 - z^2 = 0 \implies (y - z)(y + z) = 0 \implies y = \pm z
$$

This is a pair of straight lines intersecting at the origin $(0, 0, 0)$ at angles of $45^\circ$ to the coordinate axes.

#### Cross-Section $y = 0$ ($xz$-plane)
Substitute $y = 0$:

$$
x^2 = z^2 \implies x^2 - z^2 = 0 \implies (x - z)(x + z) = 0 \implies x = \pm z
$$

This is a pair of straight lines intersecting at the origin $(0, 0, 0)$ at angles of $45^\circ$ to the coordinate axes.

#### Qualitative Shape
The surface is a double circular cone consisting of two nappes meeting at the apex $(0, 0, 0)$:
- An upper cone opening upwards for $z \ge 0$,
- A lower cone opening downwards for $z \le 0$.

---

### 2. Analysis of $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ (Circular Cylinder)

#### Cross-Sections $z = c$
Substitute $z = c$ into the equation:

$$
x^2 + y^2 = 4 = 2^2
$$

For every value of $c \in \mathbb{R}$, this is a circle lying in the plane $z = c$, centered at $(0, 0, c)$, with constant radius

$$
R = 2
$$

The size of the cross-section does not change with height.

#### Cross-Section $x = 0$ ($yz$-plane)
Substitute $x = 0$:

$$
y^2 = 4 \implies y = \pm 2
$$

In the $yz$-plane, this represents two parallel vertical lines: $y = 2$ and $y = -2$, both parallel to the $z$-axis.

#### Cross-Section $y = 0$ ($xz$-plane)
Substitute $y = 0$:

$$
x^2 = 4 \implies x = \pm 2
$$

In the $xz$-plane, this represents two parallel vertical lines: $x = 2$ and $x = -2$, both parallel to the $z$-axis.

#### Qualitative Shape
The surface is an infinite right circular cylinder of radius $2$ centered along the $z$-axis.

---

### 3. Why the Radius Depends on $z$ in the First Case but is Constant in the Second

#### Algebraic Perspective
- In the cone equation $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$, the vertical coordinate $z$ is explicitly present. Fixing $z = c$ sets the value of the sum of squares $x^2 + y^2$ directly equal to $c^2$, making the radius $R(c) = |c|$ a direct function of $z$.
- In the cylinder equation $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, the variable $z$ is completely absent (it is a free variable). Consequently, no matter what value $z$ takes, the condition on $(x, y)$ remains strictly $x^2 + y^2 = 4$.

#### Geometric and Homogeneity Perspective
- **Homogeneity of the Cone:**
  The equation $x^2 + y^2 - z^2 = 0$ is homogeneous of degree $2$. If a point $(x, y, z)$ lies on the surface, then for any real scalar $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$, the scaled point $(\lambda x, \lambda y, \lambda z)$ also lies on the surface. Geometrically, this means the surface consists of straight lines (rulings) passing through the origin. Scaling $z$ by a factor $\lambda$ necessarily scales the horizontal cross-section dimensions by $|\lambda|$.
- **Translational Symmetry of the Cylinder:**
  Because $z$ does not appear in $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, if $(x, y, 0)$ is on the curve, the line through $(x, y, 0)$ parallel to the $z$-axis lies entirely on the surface. The cylinder is generated by translating a circle of radius $2$ along the entire $z$-axis, which ensures that every horizontal slice is identical.
